# 10 Major Cyberattacks and Data Breaches in 2025 (So Far)

The CRN article highlights significant cybersecurity incidents that have disrupted various sectors in 2025. Here’s a summary of the key events:

1. **Microsoft SharePoint Server Exploits**  
A widespread campaign, known as "ToolShell," targeted on-premises SharePoint servers, exploiting zero-day vulnerabilities. Over 400 systems, including U.S. government agencies, were compromised, with some attacks linked to China-based threat actors.

2. **SafePay Ransomware Attacks**  
The emerging ransomware group SafePay launched multiple attacks, including a notable one against IT distributor Ingram Micro during the July 4 holiday. These incidents disrupted operations and highlighted the growing threat from new cybercriminal groups.

3. **Ivanti VPN Vulnerability**  
A critical zero-day vulnerability in Ivanti's Connect Secure VPN was exploited, allowing remote code execution without authentication. The flaw affected organizations like Nominet and was linked to a China-based threat actor.

4. **Oracle E-Business Suite Extortion**  
The Clop ransomware group targeted Oracle's E-Business Suite customers, exploiting unpatched vulnerabilities to steal sensitive data. The group then extorted victims by threatening to release the stolen information.

5. **Red Hat GitLab Breach**  
Red Hat's consulting division suffered a breach in its self-managed GitLab instance, compromising project specifications and internal communications. While no sensitive personal data was identified, the incident raised concerns about supply chain security.

The major cyberattacks and data breaches in 2025 show why cybersecurity is more important than ever. Many attacks happened because companies did not update or fix known problems in their software and devices, showing the need for regular updates and patches. Some attacks were carried out by state-sponsored hackers, meaning countries are increasingly involved in cyberattacks against governments and businesses. Important sectors like government offices, food distributors, and IT service companies are especially at risk, which can cause big problems if attacked. Ransomware groups like SafePay and Clop are becoming more skilled, using advanced methods to steal data and demand money. These events also show that traditional security methods, like VPNs, are not enough, and companies need cloud-based and zero-trust systems. Overall, these attacks highlight the need for careful planning, constant monitoring, and building security into software from the start to protect people and organizations from cyber threats.
