# Major Cybersecurity Incident: Kido International Nursery Hack (2025)

## Key Points

- A cyberattack targeted **Kido International**, a childcare provider in London, and hackers claim to have stolen data on over **8,000 children** who attend Kido’s nurseries.  
  *Source: Reuters*

- The data published included **names, photos, home addresses, and family contact information** for at least 10 children as proof of the breach.  
  *Source: Reuters*

- The hacker group calling themselves **Radiant** claimed responsibility. On their leak site, they threatened to release more profiles (reportedly **30 per child and 100 employees**) if demands were not met.  
  *Source: Reuters*

- The hackers said they had been inside Kido’s network for **weeks** prior to the public disclosure.  
  *Source: Reuters*

- The hackers said they were based in **Russia**, but provided no evidence for this claim.  
  *Source: Reuters*

- **Kido International** did not publicly respond (at least in the article) to requests for comment.  
  *Source: Reuters*

- The **Metropolitan Police** in London said no arrests had been made yet. Investigations by its Cyber Crime Unit are in early stages.  
  *Source: Reuters*

- The article notes that **ransomware and data breaches** have been rising in the UK, hitting major businesses and even critical services.  
  *Source: Reuters*

- It also references related incidents:
  - A ransomware attack on **Collins Aerospace** disrupted airport systems (Heathrow & others).
  - Earlier attacks on British retailers (e.g. **Marks & Spencer**) caused major financial damage.  
  *Source: Reuters*

- A cybersecurity expert from the **UK National Cyber Security Centre** said that attacking organizations caring for children is especially egregious, warning that *“cybercriminals will target anyone if they think there is money to be made.”*  
  *Source: Reuters*

---

## Why It’s Significant / Possible Angles for the Project

- **Vulnerability of “soft targets”**: Nurseries are not typical high-risk enterprises like banks, but they hold sensitive personal data (especially about children), making them attractive to attackers.

- **Ethical & reputational stakes**: The breach involves highly vulnerable individuals (children), magnifying public outrage, legal liability, and trust issues.

- **Attackers’ strategy**: Holding back some data and threatening to release more — a form of **double extortion** or data leak coercion.

- **Detection & dwell time**: Hackers were inside the system for weeks — shows how infiltration and staying hidden are key challenges.

- **Response & accountability**: Limited comment from Kido and no arrests yet — highlights the difficulty in attribution, enforcement, and crisis management.

- **Broader trend**: Fits into a rising pattern of ransomware and extortion attacks in the UK affecting businesses, infrastructure, and supply chains.
